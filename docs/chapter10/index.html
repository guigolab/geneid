Chapter 10

History & references

Three decades of geneid, its authors, and how to cite it.

A short history

The first geneid was developed at the Molecular Biology Computer Research Resource (Dana-Farber Cancer Institute / Harvard) by Roderic Guigó, Steen Knudsen, Neil Drake and Temple F. Smith. It was never distributed, but an e-mail server was later set up at the Biomolecular Engineering Research Center (Boston University) by Kathleen Klose and Steen Knudsen.

geneid was rewritten from scratch at the Institut Municipal d'Investigació Mèdica (IMIM) in Barcelona in late 1999, mostly by Enrique Blanco and Roderic Guigó, with contributions from Moisés Burset and Xavier Messeguer. That version, v1.0, was released in July 2000. v1.1 (2002) added XML output, ORF searching and homology support and roughly doubled the speed; v1.2 (2004) added multi-FASTA processing, forcing a complete gene, and more species.

Development later moved to the Centre for Genomic Regulation (CRG) in Barcelona, where Tyler Alioto took on maintenance and continued development. The modern releases add substantial capability:

EraWhat changed
v1.4.xGrow-on-demand memory (no build-time capacity profiles); build and portability cleanups.
v1.5.xA Python geneid-train trainer; U2 branch-point modelling; U12 (minor-spliceosome) intron prediction and typing; a soft intron-length model.
v1.6.0Direct indexed evidence — bigWig / bigBed, and (with htslib) BAM coverage and junctions; a statistically grounded RNA‑seq expression score (depth-invariant per-base log-likelihood ratio) with UTR calling and multi-library combining; GFF3 output.

On the name

Over the years the program has been written GeneID, GeneId, geneID and Geneid. Since the 1.0 release the canonical spelling — the one most in keeping with UNIX command names — is simply geneid.

Authors

geneid v1.6 is developed by Enrique Blanco, Tyler Alioto and Roderic Guigó at the CRG. Parameter files have been created by Genís Parra, Francisco Câmara and Tyler Alioto, with earlier contributions from Moisés Burset and Xavier Messeguer. Questions and bug reports are welcome at geneid@crg.es.

How to cite

For general use, cite the Genome Research paper and the Current Protocols chapter:

  • G. Parra, E. Blanco and R. Guigó. geneid in Drosophila. Genome Research 10:511–515, 2000.
  • T. Alioto, E. Blanco, G. Parra and R. Guigó. Using geneid to Identify Genes. Current Protocols in Bioinformatics 64(1):e56, 2018. doi:10.1002/cpbi.56. PMID: 30332532.
